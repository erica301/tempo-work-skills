# Extraction map

Your job is to pull out what the sources actually say about a question, quoted, so I can see where every point comes from before anything is written up. Most of the trust in AI work comes from this step. A summary written straight from documents reads well and can't be checked; a map can be.

## When to skip the table

If the answer is one fact from one document ("what's the deadline in this call?"), quote the line, name the document and section, and stop. No table.

## How to do it

1. **Name the question.** Say in one line what you're answering. If my request is vague ("summarise these"), ask me once what I need the summary for, because that decides what's relevant.
2. **List the sources you'll read.** Name each document. If there are more than 10, ask me which matter most before reading them all.
3. **Go through each source, one at a time.** Read it in full. Don't rely on a document's own summary, title or your memory of it.
4. **Build the table.** One row per item that bears on the question:

| # | Passage, quoted word for word | Document and section | Why it matters to the question |
|---|---|---|---|

   - Quote exactly. Short quotes are better than paraphrase.
   - Give the section, page or heading so I can find it.
   - Where the sources don't cover something the question needs, add a row that says **not in the sources** and what is missing.
5. **Then the conclusion, on top.** Two or three lines above the table, citing row numbers ("Rows 2 and 5 say it's a procurement, not a sub-grant"). If the rows don't support a conclusion, say so.

## Rules

- Never fill a gap from general knowledge. "Not in the sources" is a correct answer. If I ask for general context as well, keep it in a separate section headed "Not from your documents".
- Never quote a passage you didn't read in this conversation.
- If a document wouldn't open, say which one and carry on with the rest.
- Keep my wording for the question and the document names.
- End with one line: "Open one or two of these rows in the source before you rely on them."
