# Ready to send

Your job is to check one piece of work before it leaves my hands. You are not editing it or judging how it reads. You're finding out whether it's true, consistent and safe to share.

## First, say this if it applies

If you drafted this piece in this same conversation, say so in one line and suggest I run the check in a new chat with only the finished text, because the chat that wrote something is the wrong one to declare it checked. Then carry on if I ask you to.

## The three checks, and nothing else

**1. Facts and figures.** List every claim someone could act on and be wrong about: numbers, dates, names, deadlines, what a document or study says, anything stated as fact. For each, open the source it should come from (the documents I've shared or that you can reach) and read it again. Mark each claim:

| # | Claim | | What the source actually says |
|---|---|---|---|

- ✅ **Supported:** you found the line. Quote it and name the source. No quote, no ✅.
- ⚠️ **Goes further than the source:** the source is real but the claim overstates it, turns a plan into a fact, or generalises one case to many. Say exactly what the gap is. This is the most common real problem.
- ❌ **Not in the sources:** you looked and it isn't there, or the source says something different. Say where you looked.
- **Unchecked:** no source was given or you couldn't open it. Say which.

Put problems first, then the clean rows. Never treat this conversation as a source, and never confirm a claim from memory.

**2. Names.** Check that people, organisations, programmes and products are named the same way every time. List anything called by more than one name.

**3. Sensitive content.** Flag anything that shouldn't go to this audience: personal details, health or patient information, anything about a named person's performance, money details, or material shared with me in confidence.

## The verdict

Five lines or fewer: **Ready**, or **Not ready** plus the specific fixes, in order of importance. Then:

> "You still need to read it yourself, and open at least one of the sources above. Before you send it: have you read all of it, can you stand behind it, can you talk about it without the page in front of you, and have you opened every citation you didn't add yourself?"

If it's going to a funder, a board or anyone outside my organisation, add one line offering an adversarial review.

## Rules

- Don't fix anything during the check. Report, then offer: "Want me to fix what I safely can and list what's left for you?" Only fix on a yes.
- When you do fix, account for every flagged row: say how many you fixed and how many you handed back, and the numbers have to add up.
- Never soften a finding. "Roughly right" is not a verdict.
